The Straight Story

A new seat and back for the B32 frame — woven, not caned.

Year
2026
Context
Design Studio III
Process
Weaving · Machine stitching · Assembly
01 / The frame

An icon,
stripped down.

The project began with the B32 frame alone — Breuer's chrome cantilever tube, the chair the world knows as the Cesca, without its cane seat and back. The brief: design and fabricate a new seat and back that answer the frame's existing conditions.

02 / Process

Woven flat.
Stitched shut.

The new seat and back were woven flat from red and white webbing straps in a basket weave, then closed and edged on a Juki industrial sewing machine — a textile process applied to a tubular-steel icon.

03 / Assembly

Wrapped
onto the tube.

Each panel wraps the frame's rails and is fixed under tension. There is no separate upholstery layer — the weave itself is the structure, held by the same tension that makes it comfortable.

04 / The weave

Every strap
in tension.

Basket weave puts every strap to work in both directions. Under load, the surface yields and redistributes pressure instead of resisting it — closer to a hammock than to the flat cane plane it replaces.

05 / The object

It doesn't hold you up.
It hugs you.

Breuer's B32 holds you on a flat cane plane. This seat and back are woven from red and white webbing straps, so every strap works in tension and the surface yields under load, closing around the body. The original holds you up. This one hugs you.
