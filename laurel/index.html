01 / The role

From design information to a visual conversation.

As a Creative Design Intern at The Laurel Group, I collaborated with landscape architects, designers, and other team members to produce landscape renderings. I interpreted supplied plans, analyzed proposal details, and incorporated feedback to develop and refine the images.

My renderings supported the team’s sales process and helped sell projects. The studies below connect the source information to the resulting images: site photographs establish context, plans and specifications explain the intent, and focused visual studies make the proposals easier to discuss.

My role: AI research, workflow integration, plan and proposal interpretation, and visualization. Landscape design direction, plans, and supplied site references: The Laurel Group design team. Renderings are AI-assisted concept studies.

01 / Visualization study

Poolside Terrace

Contract review → height correction → revised views

Read the full study

For Poolside Terrace, I analyzed a contractor’s proposal and caught a planter-height error that had carried into an early rendering. Site photographs, supplied markups, and a planting-plan detail helped connect the written specifications to the intended design.

The contract incorrectly listed one planter at 28″ high. Both planters were intended to be 18″ high, with different lengths. I identified the discrepancy during my proposal review and corrected the height in the later renderings.

Corrected design intent

02 / Visualization study

Layered Arrival

Site context → planting plan → layered landscape

Read the full study

The curved drive, lawn, fence, and gates establish the setting for this planting study. My visualization work focused on making the proposed planting readable within that existing setting—showing layers and enclosure while keeping the route to the gate clear.

The supplied landscape-enhancement plan locates planting beds around the drive and house. The detail calls out 4 Sweetbay magnolias, 4 Osmanthus ‘Gulftide’, 27 Hydrangea ‘Summer Crush’, and 29 TP fountain grasses.

03 / Visualization study

Garden dining

Site and CAD references → outdoor gathering spaces

Read the full study

The existing garden has a timber outbuilding, mature planting, and an informal lawn. The supplied CAD plan adds a clear spatial idea: a dining area held by perimeter planting, connected to the rest of the garden by stepping stones.

The dining concept uses a gravel surface to establish the gathering area. Flowering trees frame the table, while lower planting softens the boundary between the dining space and lawn.

04 / Visualization study

Pergola terrace

Supplied layout → structure, seating, and planting

Read the full study

The existing terrace and surrounding garden set the boundaries for this study. My role was to visualize the supplied pergola concept in that setting, bringing the structure, seating, and planted edges into a coherent view.

The supplied plan labels an 18′7″ × 19′10″ pergola alongside a terrace extension, stepping stones, and a TV wall. It also identifies a future hot-tub area and a planter with a lattice and water feature.

05 / Visualization study

Entry materials

Site and walkway plan → brick and stone comparisons

Read the full study

The front porch is one part of a longer approach. The supplied site photo establishes the existing architecture, while the plan shows how the proposed walkway connects to the porch and adjacent circulation.

The brick study continues a herringbone pattern onto the porch within a gray border. The stone study changes the porch to a gray paved surface while retaining a brick approach. The paired studies communicate alternatives for discussion; no final material selection is claimed.

03 / The workflow

Research into practice.
A shared workflow.

A substantial part of my internship involved researching how AI could be integrated into the company’s workflow. That research helped move AI approaches into practical use alongside my rendering work.

The internship documentation connects image preparation, compositing, refinement, and review. A plant-image catalog and written walkthroughs support consistency across projects, while communication with landscape architects, designers, and other team members keeps the images connected to the intended design.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Read the site, supplied plans, and relevant proposal details. Identify what the image needs to explain.

  2. 02

    Develop

    Compose planting, structures, and materials in Photoshop, using AI tools to explore and refine the scene.

  3. 03

    Review

    Review the image with the team. Compare it with the intended design and use feedback, annotations, and focused views to guide revisions.
